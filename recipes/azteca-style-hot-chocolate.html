Thick Azteca-style hot chocolate (less sweet)

Status: Proposed — inspired by San Churro's Azteca, not an official copy.
Makes: Two approximately 200 mL servings.
Time: 10–12 minutes.

Target texture and flavour

Rich, thick but still flowing hot chocolate with cinnamon and a mild chilli warmth. It should coat a spoon, pour in a continuous stream and thicken somewhat as it cools, without setting like custard. Less sweet than the café version.

Ingredients

  • 400 mL full-cream milk
  • 50 g 70% dark chocolate, finely chopped
  • 12 g unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 10 g cornflour (cornstarch)
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Tiny pinch of cayenne chilli (start very small)
  • Tiny pinch of salt
  • 0–10 g sugar, optional; start with none

Method

  1. In a small saucepan, whisk the cornflour, cocoa powder and cinnamon with about 100 mL of the cold milk until smooth.
  2. Whisk in the remaining milk. Heat over medium-low heat, stirring continuously.
  3. As the mixture approaches a simmer, stir continuously for 1–2 minutes to gelatinise the cornflour and develop a lightly thickened consistency. Avoid vigorous boiling.
  4. Reduce heat to low. Add the chopped chocolate and stir until fully melted and smooth.
  5. Add the cayenne and salt. Taste; add sugar only if necessary.
  6. Serve hot. It should remain pourable and thicken slightly while cooling. Add a little milk if it becomes too thick.

Adjustments for different outcome

  • Too thick or gelatinous: use 2-3g less cornflour next time, or thin with milk.
  • Too thin: increase cornflour by 2–3 g next time (disperse in cold liquid before heating).
  • Not chocolatey enough: increase cocoa powder by about 5 g.
  • Too bitter: add 5–10 g sugar or try 60% chocolate.
  • Too much cinnamon: halve the cinnamon.
  • Not enough chilli warmth: increase cayenne by a tiny amount.
