Lightly pan-fried salmon with creamy potato mash and broad beans

Status: Tested — family enjoyed the salmon, mash and broad beans combination.
Serves: Two adults, a teenager and a five-year-old (approximately 3½ adult-equivalent portions).
Time: About 35–40 minutes.

Ingredients

  • 700–900 g high-quality fresh salmon fillet, portioned into 4 pieces of similar thickness (skin on or off)
  • 1–1.2 kg potatoes, peeled or scrubbed and cut into chunks
  • Fresh broad beans already on hand, approximately 300–500 g in pods, or enough to serve as a side (use whatever quantity is available)
  • 1–2 carrots and 1 bay leaf, optional, to flavour the water for the potatoes; remove before mashing
  • 50–100 mL cream, warmed
  • 30–50 g butter, divided between fish, mash and beans
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • ½–1 lemon
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Optional: fresh dill or parsley

Method

  1. Put potato chunks in salted cold water with a bay leaf and optional carrot. Bring to a boil and simmer for 15–20 minutes, until tender.
  2. Meanwhile pod the broad beans. Drop the beans into a separate pot of boiling salted water for around 2–3 minutes, then drain and cool briefly. If beans are large or mature, squeeze each from its pale outer skin; small tender beans may not need peeling.
  3. Pat salmon dry and portion into pieces of as similar a thickness as practical. If fillets vary noticeably in thickness, cook the thicker pieces first and add thinner pieces later, or remove thinner pieces earlier. Season lightly with salt and pepper. For delicate, moist salmon, use medium or medium-low heat rather than aggressive searing.
  4. Heat olive oil in a non-stick or well-seasoned frying pan. If the salmon has skin, start skin-side down for around 4–6 minutes, depending on thickness. Turn and cook another 1–3 minutes. For skinless pieces, cook gently for approximately 3–4 minutes on the first side and 2–3 minutes on the second. Add a little butter near the end and spoon it over the fish. Adjust times for thickness and desired doneness; avoid drying it out. Food-safety needs may require a more thorough cook.
  5. Drain potatoes thoroughly, remove the bay leaf and carrots, and mash with warmed cream and some butter. Taste and season.
  6. Quickly warm the prepared broad beans with a little butter, salt and a squeeze of lemon. Serve immediately with salmon, mash and optional fresh herbs.

Family notes

  • The salmon should be lightly cooked and moist, not strongly browned or overdone.
  • Broad beans were tried and worked well for variety, but are not a default side because of variable availability. Use them when in season or already available; other easy sides include green beans, peas, or cucumber and tomato salad.
  • The family has already tested and enjoyed potato mash made with a bay leaf and carrot in the cooking water, with carrot removed before mashing.
  • Fresh salmon is best bought close to the day of cooking, with the use-by date and proper chilled storage in mind.
  • Family feedback: Dish was good overall; salmon is a reliable family favourite. The main practical challenge was uneven fillet thickness making even cooking harder. Try staggered starts or removing thinner portions earlier next time.
  • Tested with broad beans, but treat them as a seasonal variation rather than a standard ingredient.
