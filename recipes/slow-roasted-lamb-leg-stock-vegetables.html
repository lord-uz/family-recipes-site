Fall-apart slow-roasted lamb leg with stock and vegetables

Status: Proposed, not yet family-tested.
Serves: Family of four, potentially with leftovers; the exact yield depends on whether the 1.6 kg leg is bone-in.
Time: Allow about 5–5½ hours overall, including ~60 minutes active preparation and the final vegetable stage. The meat can take longer; tenderness, not the clock, is the endpoint.

Ingredients

  • 1 lamb leg, approximately 1.6 kg (bone-in or boneless)
  • 300–400 mL beef, chicken or lamb stock, using leftover stock first
  • 1–1.3 kg potatoes, cut into large chunks
  • 5–8 shallots, peeled (or 2 onions, quartered)
  • 3–4 carrots, cut into large pieces
  • ½ cauliflower, cut into large florets
  • Optional: 1 zucchini, cut into thick pieces for the final 20–30 minutes
  • 4 cloves garlic, sliced or crushed
  • 2 sprigs rosemary (or 1–2 tsp dried rosemary)
  • Optional: a few sprigs thyme
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Optional: ½ lemon to brighten the pan juices at the end

Method

  1. Prepare: Preheat the oven to 145–150°C conventional (about 135–140°C fan-forced, adjusting for your oven). Pat the lamb dry. Rub with olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic and rosemary.
  2. Set up the stock roast: Put lamb in a roomy roasting dish. Add 300–400 mL stock around, not over, the meat and cover very tightly with foil or a well-fitting lid. Avoid pouring liquid over the seasoned surface.
  3. Slow-roast: Roast covered for about 3½–4 hours before checking. Keep the dish moist, topping up with a small splash of stock or water if it is drying out. A 1.6 kg leg may need 4–5 hours or more in total for the meat to pull apart easily. If the leg is still firm, cover and continue cooking; don't force the timing.
  4. Add firm vegetables: When the lamb is getting close to tender, arrange potatoes, carrots and shallots around it, tossing in some pan juices and a little salt. Cover and return to the oven for approximately 60–90 minutes. If the pan is crowded, use a second tray with some cooking juices or oil.
  5. Finish and brown: Once the lamb pulls apart easily, move it to a covered warm platter to rest. Increase oven heat to around 200–210°C. Uncover the vegetables and roast until golden, usually another 15–25 minutes. Add cauliflower for the last 20–30 minutes, and optional zucchini towards the end. Add a splash more stock if the juices are drying out or burning.
  6. Serve: Pull the tender lamb into chunks, discard rosemary stems and serve with vegetables and spoonfuls of pan juices. Taste the juices before adding salt; a little lemon can balance the richness.

Practical timing and texture notes

  • This is meant to be fall-apart lamb, not a pink, sliceable roast. Leg is leaner than shoulder and may need patience to become shreddable; check tenderness with two forks, not just internal temperature.
  • Don't put all vegetables in at the beginning: potatoes and carrots would become too soft over the entire lamb cooking period.
  • A tightly covered roast in stock keeps moisture in the dish; uncover only for the last browning stage.
  • For a Saturday dinner, begin the lamb early afternoon so the meat can finish without rushing.
  • If the leg is bone-in, the edible meat from 1.6 kg may not be enough for two full family dinners. Keep an easy supplementary side or another meal available for Sunday.
  • Cool leftovers promptly, refrigerate, and reheat thoroughly.
