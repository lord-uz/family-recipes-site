Crème brûlée — seven egg yolks

Status: Proposed, not yet tested by the family.
Makes: 6 small portions (roughly 120 mL custard each, depending on ramekin size).
Time: Approximately 20 minutes active, 35–50 minutes baking, at least 4 hours chilling.
Egg-yolk use: All 7 yolks remaining from a batch of amaretti.

Ingredients

  • 7 large egg yolks
  • 650 mL thickened cream (preferably plain cream without added flavours)
  • 75 g caster sugar
  • 1 vanilla bean, split and scraped, or 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • Small pinch of salt
  • 30–45 g caster sugar for caramelising the tops (about 1–1½ tsp per ramekin)

Method

  1. Heat oven to 150°C conventional or 135–140°C fan-forced. Place six heatproof ramekins in a deep roasting dish. Boil water for the water bath.
  2. Warm the cream with the vanilla bean and its seeds over medium-low heat until steaming, not boiling. Turn off heat and let infuse for 5 minutes. If using extract, add it after heating.
  3. Whisk yolks, 75 g sugar and salt gently until combined; avoid whipping in excessive air.
  4. Slowly whisk the warm cream into the yolks a little at a time. Strain through a fine sieve into a pouring jug; skim away bubbles.
  5. Divide between ramekins. Pour hot water into the roasting dish until it reaches about halfway up their sides. Carefully move to oven.
  6. Bake 35–50 minutes, depending on dish depth, until edges are set but centres still wobble slightly. For precision, aim for approximately 78–82°C at the centre rather than letting the custard boil or become firm.
  7. Lift ramekins out of the water bath, cool briefly and refrigerate at least 4 hours (preferably overnight).
  8. Just before serving, blot any condensation from the tops, scatter a thin, even layer of sugar over each and caramelise with a kitchen blowtorch. Let the crust harden for 1–2 minutes.

Notes

  • A blowtorch gives more even caramelisation than an oven grill; a very hot grill can work but may warm the custard.
  • For a less sweet result, the custard can be tried with 60 g sugar, but retain some surface sugar to make a crisp shell.
  • The aim is a smooth, spoonable custard with a thin, brittle caramel top. Do not bake until completely solid.
  • Since the mixture contains egg and dairy, chill promptly after cooling and keep refrigerated.
  • Pairs with: Soft amaretti cookies, although both are desserts.
