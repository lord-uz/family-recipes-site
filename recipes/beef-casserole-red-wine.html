Slow-cooked beef casserole with Cabernet Sauvignon

Status: Proposed / not yet evaluated by the family.
Serves: Approximately seven adult-equivalent portions (two family dinners).
Active preparation: About 60 minutes. Total: Approximately 3–3½ hours.
Variation of: Slow-cooked beef and vegetable casserole.

Ingredients

  • 1.2–1.4 kg diced chuck beef (FoodWorks approximate cut weight)
  • 250 mL dry Cabernet Sauvignon
  • 350–450 mL gluten-free beef stock, plus extra if required
  • 2 brown onions, sliced or diced
  • 5 carrots, chopped
  • 1.2 kg potatoes, cut into large pieces
  • 3 cloves garlic, chopped
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste (optional; check gluten-free suitability)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Optional: thyme
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 155–160°C. Pat the beef dry. Brown in batches in a heavy oven-safe casserole or frying pan, then set aside.
  2. In the same pan, soften onions and carrots for a few minutes. Add garlic and optional tomato paste, cooking briefly without burning.
  3. Deglaze with wine: Pour in 250 mL Cabernet Sauvignon and scrape up the browned bits from the pan. Simmer uncovered for approximately 5–10 minutes. A reduction of roughly 50–100 mL is optional, not a target; adjust to flavour and convenience. Reducing wine does not guarantee removal of all alcohol.
  4. Return the beef and add 350–450 mL stock, bay leaves, thyme if using, and a little pepper. Transfer to an oven-safe casserole if needed. The liquid should partly surround, not completely submerge, the meat.
  5. Cover tightly and bake for approximately 2½–3 hours, checking tenderness and liquid level. Add more water or gluten-free stock only if needed. Allow longer if the beef is still firm.
  6. Add the potatoes during the final 45–60 minutes, cooking until tender. Adjust seasoning at the end.
  7. If the sauce is too thin, uncover and simmer briefly or add a small slurry of verified gluten-free cornflour. Do not use ordinary wheat flour.
  8. Serve one family dinner; cool remaining portions promptly in shallow containers, refrigerate and reheat thoroughly for the next dinner.

Planning and family notes

  • Budget 60 minutes of active cooking when preparing solo at a relaxed pace; do not assume vegetables can be prepped while browning meat. Two cooks or better heat output may shorten this.

  • Suggested for a longer cooking session, with leftovers for another day.

  • This is a wine variation; retain the original stock-only version as a separate recipe.
  • Wine contributes acidity and depth, but some alcohol may remain even after uncovered reduction and covered oven cooking. Do not present the dish as alcohol-free.
  • The 250 mL wine plus 350–450 mL stock is the starting liquid budget. Add stock based on the actual reduction, pan size, vegetables and evaporation rather than trying to preserve an exact ratio.
  • The recipe worked well and wine adds new taste to the casserole.
