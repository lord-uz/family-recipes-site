Pan-fried barramundi with creamy potato mash

Status: Family-tested — fish and mash were excellent. Serves: Approximately 3.5 adult portions.
Accompaniments: Flexible; roasted broccoli was tried but needs refinement.

Main ingredients

  • 2 thawed skinless barramundi side fillets (portion into similar-thickness pieces)
  • About 1–1.2 kg potatoes
  • 1–2 carrots, boiled with the potatoes for flavour then removed before mashing
  • 1 bay leaf, also removed before mashing
  • Cream, approx. 50mL
  • About 20–30 g butter for the fish, plus optional butter for mash
  • 1–2 tbsp olive oil for frying
  • ½–1 lemon
  • Salt and pepper
  • Optional: garlic and fresh dill or parsley

Method

  1. Cut potatoes into similar-size pieces and simmer with the carrot(s), bay leaf and salt until tender, approximately 15–20 minutes. Remove carrots and bay leaf before mashing. Drain well, mash with warmed cream and optional butter, season to taste and keep warm.
  2. Pat thawed skinless barramundi dry. Cut into similar-thickness portions for even cooking; season with salt and pepper.
  3. Heat olive oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Fry fish about 3–4 minutes on the first side without moving, adjusting for thickness.
  4. Turn carefully, reduce heat to medium and add butter and optional garlic. Cook another approximately 2–3 minutes while spooning butter over the fish. Add fresh dill or parsley in the last 30 seconds; finish with lemon.
  5. Ensure fish is cooked through (63°C in the thickest portion is a conservative target) and serve immediately with hot creamy mash.

Optional vegetable accompaniments

  • Roasted broccoli — tried, still experimenting: Toss florets with olive oil, salt, pepper, optional paprika/garlic powder. Roast spaced out at 210–220°C for approximately 15–20 minutes. The family found it a good complement to mash, but the result could be improved.
  • Steamed green beans — proposed: Finish with a little butter and lemon.
  • Peas — proposed: Quick and convenient from frozen.
  • Cucumber and tomato salad — tested, go-to option with potato mash: A fresh contrast to the buttery fish and creamy mash.

Family feedback

  • Fish: great, using skinless portions of similar thickness.
  • Mash: great. Carrots and bay leaf were included during boiling; carrots were removed, not mashed into potatoes. Cream added after draining.
  • Roasted broccoli: complements the mash but preparation needs further experimentation.
  • Cucumber and tomato salad is the family's go-to accompaniment with potato mash.
  • Keep the dish centred on barramundi plus potato mash, with interchangeable vegetable sides.

Coeliac and planning notes

  • Main recipe is naturally gluten-free if ingredients and shared utensils are suitable.
  • Avoid pre-crumbed/pre-marinated fish unless verified gluten-free.
  • Buy fresh fish near cooking or thaw frozen fish safely in the fridge.
