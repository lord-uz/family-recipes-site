Lemon curd — four egg yolks

Status: Proposed, not yet tested by the family.
Yield: Approximately 350–400 g, depending on reduction.
Time: 15–20 minutes cooking, plus cooling and refrigeration.
Egg-yolk use: 4 yolks; pairs with a three-yolk hollandaise or carbonara to use the seven yolks from amaretti.

Ingredients

  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 120 mL freshly squeezed lemon juice (usually 2–3 lemons)
  • Finely grated zest of 2 lemons (avoid white pith)
  • 130 g caster sugar (reduce slightly after the first trial if preferred)
  • 90 g unsalted butter, diced
  • Small pinch of salt

Method

  1. In a heatproof bowl or small heavy-bottomed saucepan, whisk together the yolks, sugar, lemon juice, zest and salt.
  2. Cook gently over a pan of barely simmering water (bowl not touching water), stirring continuously and scraping the base and sides. Alternatively use a heavy-based saucepan over very low heat.
  3. Heat until noticeably thickened and able to coat a spoon, ideally around 75–80°C. Do not let it boil; excessive heat may scramble the eggs.
  4. Remove from heat and stir in the butter a few pieces at a time until fully incorporated.
  5. For an especially smooth curd, pass through a fine sieve. Transfer to a clean container, cover and refrigerate as it cools; it will thicken further.
  6. Serve chilled with amaretti, yoghurt, pancakes, toast, fruit or as a cake filling.

Notes

  • This version uses yolks only, giving a rich, smooth curd rather than a lighter whole-egg curd.
  • Sweetness: Start with 130 g sugar and adjust next time; reducing sugar also changes the balance and body. Lemon acidity varies.
  • Texture: The curd should be pourable when hot and softly spreadable once chilled; don't overcook it into a stiff paste.
  • Refrigerate promptly and use within about one week for best quality; freeze portions if not using soon.
  • Pairs with: Soft amaretti cookies and smoked-salmon eggs Benedict (4 + 3 yolks).
