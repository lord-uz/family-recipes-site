Smoked salmon eggs Benedict with hollandaise

Status: Proposed, not yet tested by the family.
Serves: 4 (one poached egg per person; scale up for larger appetites).
Time: Approximately 30–35 minutes.
Leftover-yolk use: 3 yolks in the hollandaise, alongside 4 whole eggs for poaching. This pairs well with four-yolk lemon curd.

Ingredients

Eggs Benedict

  • 4 large eggs, for poaching
  • 2 English muffins, split into 4 halves (or toasted bread of choice)
  • 120–160 g cold-smoked salmon, divided into four portions
  • Optional: baby spinach, chives or dill
  • Optional: a splash of white vinegar in the poaching water

Hollandaise sauce

  • 3 large egg yolks
  • 150 g unsalted butter, melted and kept warm
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice (15 mL), plus more to taste
  • 1 tbsp water
  • Pinch of salt and a little white or black pepper
  • Optional tiny pinch of cayenne pepper

Method

Hollandaise

  1. Melt butter gently and keep it warm but not scorching.
  2. Whisk egg yolks, water and lemon juice in a heatproof bowl placed over a saucepan of barely simmering water; do not let the bowl touch the water.
  3. Whisk continuously for roughly 2–4 minutes until the yolks become pale, foamy and lightly thickened. Take the bowl off the heat intermittently if getting too hot.
  4. Very gradually drizzle in warm melted butter while whisking to form a thick, smooth emulsion. Season with salt, pepper and optional cayenne. If too thick, whisk in warm water 1 tsp at a time.
  5. Keep the sauce warm (not hot) while assembling, and serve promptly. Avoid overheating or it may split or curdle.

Poach and assemble

  1. Bring a wide saucepan of water to a very gentle simmer. Crack each egg into a small cup. Slip eggs into the water and poach approximately 3–4 minutes for softly set whites and runny yolks, or longer if preferred.
  2. Meanwhile, toast the muffin halves. Lay smoked salmon over each half; add spinach if desired.
  3. Lift eggs out with a slotted spoon, drain briefly and place one on each muffin half. Spoon warm hollandaise over and finish with chives or dill. Serve immediately.

Notes

  • This is the smoked-salmon variation of eggs Benedict, often called eggs Royale.
  • Food safety: Traditional hollandaise is not necessarily fully cooked, and softly poached eggs have runny yolks. Use fresh eggs, prepare just before eating, and cook eggs/sauce more thoroughly or use pasteurised eggs for those at higher risk.
  • Troubleshooting: If the sauce starts to split, whisk a teaspoon of warm water in a clean bowl and add the split sauce gradually while whisking.
  • Practicality: Make the hollandaise close to serving time, as it does not reheat well.
  • Alternative: Hot-smoked salmon or briefly cooked fresh salmon can replace cold-smoked salmon.
